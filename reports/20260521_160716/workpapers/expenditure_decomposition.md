# Expenditure-Side GDP Decomposition — 10 Anomalous EU Countries, 2024–2025

**Prepared:** 2026-05-21  
**Data vintage:** Eurostat `nama_10_gdp`, last updated **2026-05-20** [[1]]  
**Unit:** Percentage-point (pp) contribution to real GDP growth, chain-linked volumes  
**Eurostat series code:** `CON_PPCH_PRE` (contribution to GDP growth, % point change on previous period)

---

## Component Key

| Symbol | Eurostat item code | Description |
|--------|--------------------|-------------|
| **C** | `P31_S14_S15` | Household & NPISH final consumption expenditure |
| **G** | `P3_S13` | General government final consumption expenditure |
| **I** | `P51G` | Gross fixed capital formation (GFCF) |
| **ΔInv** | `P52_P53` | Changes in inventories & acquisitions less disposals of valuables |
| **EX** | `P6` | Exports of goods & services |
| **IM** | `P7` | Imports of goods & services (Eurostat sign: negative = drag on growth) |
| **NX** | `P6 + P7` | Net exports contribution (EX + IM, Eurostat-signed) |
| **GDP** | `B1GQ` | Headline real GDP growth (%) |

> **Note on imports sign convention:** Imports are shown with their Eurostat national-accounts sign. A negative IM value means imports grew faster than exports (drag on GDP); a positive IM value means imports fell (boost to GDP). `NX = EX + IM`.

## Analytical Notes

### Ireland — Statistical distortion vs. genuine activity
Ireland's +12.34% GDP growth in 2025 is dominated by GFCF (+6.32 pp) and inventory accumulation (+4.40 pp), the combined hallmarks of intellectual property import bookings and pharma sector front-loading. The export contribution (+7.63 pp) is large but partially offset by imports (−5.90 pp). Private consumption at −0.32 pp confirms the disconnect between headline GDP and actual living standards (Modified Domestic Demand is a better welfare indicator for Ireland). [[1]]

### Germany — Structural stagnation
Germany's narrow escape from a second consecutive contraction year (+0.24%) masks ongoing structural weakness. The negative GFCF contribution (−0.04 pp) and severe NX drag (−1.53 pp) signal that neither investment recovery nor export competitiveness has been restored. Consumption growth (+0.82 pp) is the sole bright spot, likely reflecting real wage recovery. [[1]]

### Hungary / Italy — Consumption-led with investment weakness
Both Hungary and Italy show consumption-positive but NX-negative profiles in 2025. Hungary's investment contraction (−0.65 pp GFCF) is particularly concerning given the 2024 investment collapse (−2.20 pp). Italy's GFCF turned positive (+0.75 pp) in 2025, a mild reversal from 2024's −0.69 pp. [[1]]

### Luxembourg — Extreme gross trade volatility
Luxembourg's 2024 gross trade values (EX −26.48 pp, IM +25.19 pp) are statistical artefacts of its role as a financial hub — cross-border SPE and investment fund flows dominate the trade accounts in some years. The 2025 figures are more moderate (EX +2.23 pp, IM −2.87 pp) and closer to economic fundamentals. [[1]]
